How it works

  1. 1

    Pick your apps

    Choose the apps you want gone when you snib. The dialler, messages, emergency calling and Settings can never be locked.

  2. 2

    Tap to snib

    Hold your phone against a token. A sharp buzz, the mark turns, and your blocklist is shut. That's the whole action.

  3. 3

    Tap to open up

    Tap the same token again to unsnib. No passwords, no timers to wait out, no screen to argue with. The key is in your hand or it isn't.

Why a token?

Every other blocker keeps the off switch on the same screen as the block.

A dashboard, a timer, an “are you sure?”. When the urge hits, the way out is one tap away, on the glass you're already holding. Willpower against a button you put there yourself is not a fair fight.

Snib moves the switch off the screen and into the world.

To open up you need the token — on your desk, on your keys, in another room. That small physical distance is the whole product. It isn't there to defeat you with a laptop; it's there to beat the reflex.

Tokens belong to a household

Set Snib up and it writes a token for you. Any blank NFC tag — a sticker, a disc, something nicer — becomes one in a tap. Make as many as you like: one by the desk, one by the bed.

Add your partner's phone by tapping any of your tokens on it. Now your tokens work on their phone and theirs on yours. Someone else's Snib token does nothing on either.

  • No account. No email, no sign-in.
  • No server. Your household lives on your phones, nowhere else.
  • Nothing leaves the phone. Snib has no analytics and no network calls.
  • No subscription. A free app and a token you buy once.

You can always get back in

A phone must never trap you. There are three ways back in, in the order you'll reach for them.

Another token

Usually the answer. Your partner's token is on their keys; any token in your household opens any phone in it.

An emergency break

Three a week, five minutes each, resetting Monday. Deliberately scarce, never for sale. Enough to grab the thing you actually needed.

Recovery codes

Eight single-use codes from setup, kept in your wallet. For when the token's at home and you're in another city needing your banking app.

What Snib won't pretend

Snib makes your blocklist hard to undo. It does not make it impossible, and it never will claim to. A determined person with a minute and the Settings app can switch it off — the same ceiling every blocker on Android lives under. The job is beating the reflex, not beating you with a laptop. If a blocker tells you it's unbreakable, its own update history is telling you otherwise.

Private by construction

Snib asks for the Accessibility permission because that is the only way one Android app can tell when another is opening, and hold a block in front of it. It reads nothing else and sends nothing anywhere.

There's no account to leak and no server to breach, because there isn't one. Your household secret is kept in your phone's hardware keystore and backed up nowhere.

  • Open source. Every line is on GitHub.
  • Works offline. No connection needed, ever.
  • Android. NFC phone required. iOS can't block app launches the same way.

Not on the store yet.

Snib is being built and lived with before it ships. You can read the whole thing — the app, the design brief, the engineering notes — on GitHub today.
